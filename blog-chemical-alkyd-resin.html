Acid Value and Viscosity Tracking in Alkyd Resin Cooking

Why an alkyd polyesterification is followed by acid value and viscosity, what the reference methods involve, why step-growth gelation makes the endpoint so time-critical, and what peer-reviewed in-line NIR studies on polyester resins have shown — with the standards and literature behind each statement.

Why the cook is followed by two numbers

Alkyd resins are fatty-acid- or oil-modified polyesters; the Ullmann's chapter on alkyds covers raw materials, polymerisation theory, formulation and process types, and manufacture [1]. A published preparation shows the sequence used in chemical plants: soybean oil was alcoholysed with glycerol at 240 °C, phthalic anhydride was added and the polyesterification was run at 220 °C, monitored by checking acid value and viscosity at regular intervals, and ended when the acid value had fallen below 15 mg KOH/g [2].

Conversion, molecular weight and the gel point

Alkyd formation is a step-growth polymerisation. Carothers' treatment relates the number-average degree of polymerisation to the fraction of functional groups reacted: chain length rises slowly for most of the reaction and steeply in the last few per cent of conversion, and with monomers of functionality above two the same analysis predicts a critical conversion at which an infinite network forms, i.e. the batch gels [6][8]. Flory's statistical treatment showed that gelation sets in at a lower conversion than the Carothers estimate, because the largest molecules reach the network state before the average one does [7][8]. For an alkyd cooked with glycerol, a trifunctional polyol, the usable endpoint therefore lies in a narrow window close to the gel point, and overshooting turns the reactor contents into an irreversible gel. Formulators reduce the risk with chain stoppers, such as the benzoic acid used in the preparation above to consume part of the hydroxyl groups and prevent gelation [2]; process monitoring reduces it by catching the endpoint in time.

The reference methods

Acid value of polyester resins and paint binders is determined by ISO 2114, which defines it as the milligrams of potassium hydroxide needed to neutralise one gram of resin, distinguishes a partial acid value (method A, carboxyl groups plus half of any free anhydride) from a total acid value (method B, anhydrides first hydrolysed for 20 min), and states in its scope that it serves both specification testing and research use to monitor the completion of the polycondensation reaction [3]. In method A the resin is dissolved in a two-to-one toluene–ethanol mixture and titrated potentiometrically with 0.1 mol/L ethanolic potassium hydroxide [3]. Viscosity of the resin solution is measured by timing methods: ASTM D1545 times the rise of a bubble in a standard tube, reports bubble seconds, which are approximately equal to stokes for most liquids, and applies only to transparent liquids free of crystalline or gel particles [4]; ISO 2431 measures flow time through cups with 3, 4, 5 or 6 mm orifices and is usable only where the flow breakpoint can be determined with certainty, which becomes difficult towards its 100 s upper limit [5]. All of these need a sample drawn from a hot reactor, cooled, diluted to a defined solids content and measured, so the result describes the batch as it was when sampled, not as it is when the result arrives.

What in-line NIR has shown on polyester reactions

Near-infrared spectra consist mainly of overtone and combination bands of X–H vibrations, above all C–H, O–H and N–H, which makes the region sensitive to hydroxyl-containing groups such as carboxylic acids, alcohols and water [9]. Blanco et al. followed an acid-catalysed esterification in-line by NIR and resolved the spectra into concentration profiles of the reacting species by multivariate curve resolution; the profiles deviated from reference values by under 3 % and yielded the rate constant and the effect of water in the feed [10]. For polyester resins, Marengo et al. modelled hydroxyl number and acid value from FT-NIR spectra of 62 resin samples, compared neural networks with PCR and PLS, found the networks more accurate but more demanding in training data, and concluded that FT-NIR could follow both numbers during polyesterification on the process line [11]. Closest to an alkyd cook is the work of Avila et al., who placed a miniaturised MEMS Fabry–Pérot interferometer covering 1,350–1,650 nm, with an immersion transmission probe, in a 2 L reactor producing saturated polyester resin over twelve batches at 220–240 °C [12]. Temperature variation, solid particles and bubbles caused strong spectral interference, so robust pre-processing and long-term probe stability were prerequisites; with those in place, PLS models predicted acid number and viscosity, and a PCA model served for end-point detection within multivariate statistical process control [12]. Two caveats apply: the Avila process is a saturated polyester, not an alkyd with fatty-acid chains, and the Marengo models were built on resin samples rather than in a reactor; a calibration for an alkyd reactor has to be built on that reactor's own batches.

Building the calibration for a reactor

The chemometric route is the usual one, scatter correction and derivatives to remove physical effects [13], then PLS regression against reference values; three points decide whether the model survives a hot, multiphase reactor:

  • Reference values bound the model. Titrate acid value consistently by one ISO 2114 method, since methods A and B treat free anhydride differently [3]; measure viscosity at a fixed solids content and temperature by one timing method [4][5]; time-stamp each sample against the spectrum taken at the moment of sampling.
  • Pre-processing and probe stability are not optional. Temperature swings, solids and bubbles were the dominant disturbances in the reactor study by Avila et al. [12]; the pre-processing is chosen for them [13], and the probe must stay clean across many batches.
  • The endpoint is a multivariate decision. Because chain length rises steeply in the last few per cent of conversion, when few functional groups remain to be consumed [8], a whole-spectrum end-point model such as the PCA approach of Avila et al. [12] complements the predicted acid value rather than duplicating it.
