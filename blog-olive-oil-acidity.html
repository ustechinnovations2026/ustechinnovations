Free Fatty Acidity (FFA) Monitoring in Olive Oil Processing

How free fatty acidity and peroxide value define the grade of a virgin olive oil, how the IOC and ISO reference methods determine them, what peer-reviewed NIR calibrations achieve for both parameters, and where a near-infrared measurement point fits between the decanter and the storage tank.

Why acidity and peroxide value decide the grade

In the European Union the olive oil categories are defined by Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2022/2104, which replaced Regulation (EEC) No 2568/91; its Annex I limits extra virgin olive oil to a free acidity of 0.80 % (as oleic acid) and a peroxide value of 20.0 meq O2/kg, virgin olive oil to 2.0 % acidity with the same peroxide limit, and classes any virgin oil above 2.0 % acidity as lampante, which must be refined before sale [1]. The International Olive Council trade standard (COI/T.15/NC No. 3/Rev. 22, June 2026) applies the same limits in international trade [2]. For a mill in the food and feed industry these two numbers separate the oil that earns the premium from the oil that goes to the refinery.

Where free fatty acids and peroxides come from

Free acidity measures the fatty acids released from triglycerides by lipolytic enzymes present in the seed and pulp cells of the olive; when the fruit is damaged these enzymes reach the oil, so bruised or unhealthy olives and poor fruit storage are the usual causes of high acidity [3]. García and Yousfi conclude that the greatest loss of quality occurs between harvest and processing, when olives heaped in piles degrade and their oils show hydrolytic and oxidative damage: high acidity, peroxide value and ultraviolet absorbance [4]. Peroxides are the primary oxidation products, expressed as milliequivalents of active oxygen per kilogram [3].

The malaxer is a smaller lever than often assumed: with good-quality fruit, Di Giovacchino et al. found that extending malaxation from 15 to 90 minutes changed only the total phenol content, not the quality or organoleptic characteristics of the oil [5]. Acidity at the decanter outlet is therefore largely a record of fruit condition and waiting time.

The reference methods and their precision

Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2022/2105 prescribes the IOC methods: acidity by COI/T.20/Doc. No 34 (free fatty acids, cold method) and peroxide value by COI/T.20/Doc. No 35 [6]. In the cold method the oil is dissolved in diethyl ether–ethanol and the free fatty acids are titrated with potassium or sodium hydroxide, the result being expressed as per cent oleic acid [7]. For peroxide value the oil is dissolved in acetic acid and chloroform, potassium iodide is added and the liberated iodine is titrated with sodium thiosulfate [3]; the IOC standard also accepts ISO 3960 or AOCS Cd 8b-90 [2]. The general ISO methods are ISO 660:2020 [8] and ISO 3960:2017 [9].

The peroxide titration is not very precise: IOC collaborative tests gave reproducibility and repeatability coefficients of variation of 7.1 % and 1.9 % for extra virgin oil and 13.8 % and 3.4 % for ordinary olive oils, and a standard error of laboratory of 1.41 meq O2/kg has been reported [3]. A NIR calibration trained on such titrations cannot be more precise than they are.

What NIR calibrations achieve

Armenta et al. built PLS calibrations on NIR spectra of edible oils; for olive oils the validation set gave an RMSEP of 0.034 % for acidity and 1.87 meq O2/kg for peroxide value, so the method was proposed for direct quantification of acidity and screening of peroxide value, in under 30 s per sample without treatment [10]. Mailer calibrated a scanning NIR instrument with a liquid cell on 216 Australian olive oils covering virtually the range of the IOC limits and obtained an R² of 0.97 for free fatty acids [11].

Garrido-Varo et al. scanned 478 virgin olive oils in transflectance over 400–2,500 nm; in external validation on 100 oils the acidity model gave an SEP of 0.06 % with r² 0.97, the peroxide model an SEP of 1.31 meq O2/kg with r² 0.68 [12]. They attribute the weaker peroxide result to the low repeatability of the reference method and to the oxidation state changing with storage and handling before NIR analysis [12]. García Martín's 2022 review agrees: acidity and peroxide value can be measured by NIR with good precision, peroxide less precisely, and acidity models with a prediction error of about 0.34–0.35 % are too coarse against a 0.8 % limit even when their RPD exceeds 3 [3]. With a portable instrument, Garrido-Cuevas et al. reported a cross-validation RPD of 5.67 for acidity on 810 oils, against 8.45 on a benchtop analyzer [13].

Putting a measurement point in the mill

An on-line NIR sensor sampling after the clarifying centrifuge was described in 2005, and experts consulted for the 2022 review named three candidate sampling points: the horizontal decanter outlet, where results serve process adjustment rather than labelling; the outlet of the vertical centrifuge or the sedimentation tank; and the storage containers [3]. On-line installations remain limited because robust models need many samples across cultivars and harvests, and olive oil is usually measured at about 32 °C because temperature strongly affects the spectrum [3]. Three practices follow:

  • Reference data first. Titrate calibration samples by the prescribed methods [6][7], replicate the peroxide titration [3], and run NIR and titration on the same day, since the oxidation state drifts between them [12].
  • Match the calibration range to the decision. The grading thresholds are 0.80 % and 2.0 % acidity [1]; models built over a narrow, relevant range give a lower prediction error [3].
  • Treat peroxide value as a screening result. Published calibrations support quantification of acidity and screening of peroxide value [10]; a lot reading close to the 20.0 meq O2/kg limit [1] should be confirmed by titration.

How USTECH approaches this

For a flowing oil stream after the vertical centrifuge the ProLine2550 process FT-NIR analyzer is specified for liquids in pipelines; for lot-by-lot checks at the tank farm the MasterLine17EC at-line NIR analyzer is used. The caliX software provides the pre-processing and PLS regression used in the studies above [10][12], and ProChem passes the predicted values to the PLC or SCADA so that a lot can be routed to the tank matching its grade. Whether both parameters can be predicted at the precision a mill's grading decision requires is established in a feasibility study with that mill's own oils and reference titrations.
